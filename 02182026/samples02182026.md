Sample preparation

5 ml of 2% Cellulose Acetate (CA) in acetone prepared

10 mls of surfactant solution added above and below CMC so CA conc becomes 1% while rapidly stirring the solution. The CA precipitates out in the form of nanoparticles (NP). Acetone is removed via leaving the open vial stirring at room temperature overnight.

Three surfactants, SDS, TX-100 and cocoglysomate are used above and below critical micellar concentration to investigate morphological changes in NP.



Wetting experiment:



The NP samples prepared as per above methodology were dropped (4 microlitres) via a micropipette on superhydrophobic coating on glass slide from a height of **6.6 cm**. The droplet's bouncing behavior was captured via a high speed camera at above 2000 frames per second. The runs were conducted in replicates (minimum 3). 

Each sample contains water, nanoparticles and surfactant.h and l denote surfactant above and below critical micellar concentration (CMC). CMC values of each surfactant was identified from literature. For h above CMC, cocncentration 2x above CMC was used and for l concentration 0.5x times CMC was used

SDS CMC = 0.225 wt.%
TX CMC = 0.014 wt.%
CG CMC = 0.005 wt.%

Sample key

cainhcg - 1% nanoparticles in cocoglysomate solution in water above cmc (cocoglysomate conc. = 0.01 wt.%)

cainlcg - 1% nanoparticles in cocoglysomate solution in water below cmc (cocoglysomate conc. = 0.0025 wt.%)

cainhsds - 1% nanoparticles in sds solution in water above cmc (sds concentration = 0.45 wt.%)

cainlsds - 1% nanoparticles in sds solution in water below cmc (sds concentration = 0.112 wt.%)

cainhtx - 1% nanoparticles in tx-100 solution in water above cmc (tx concentration = 0.028 wt.%)

cainltx - 1% nanoparticles in tx-100 solution in water below cmc (tx concentration = 0.007 wt.%)

water - water only control

ca only -  1% ca nanoparticles in water only

Scale v - vertical ruler image with 1 graduation showing 1/16th of an inch. Image shows 12 inch mark

Scale - Horizontal ruler with 1 graduation showing 1 mm



* While the droplets were dropped from a height of 6.6 cm, the videos' frame of view only captures a small area at the bottom and not the entire 6.6 cm. This is because at the high frame rates that we are running the camera, the field of view is limited.



Magnification

The distance between the camera and the glass slide was kept same between runs to ensure uniform magnification. Also for scaling, an image of a scale was taken in both horizontal and vertical orientations to facilitate calculation of travel distances.

Parameters to be extracted:
1. Initial velocity - droplet is dropped from 6.6 cm height. Due to ejection thrust from pipette, the initial velocity may slightly differ from the free fall velocity and this can be estimated via the distance covered by the droplet between frames prior to hitting the surface and bouncinh
2. Velocity after the droplet hits the super hydrophobic coating. This will vary between samples
3. Droplet diameter before and after impact. This will vary since the droplet may fragment and part of it is left between while part of it bounces
4. The maximum spreading diameter when the droplet settles on the surface.
